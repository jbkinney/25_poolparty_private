### Capability inventory

#### 1. Three distinct library-design workflows

- **What it does:** provides SSSM/SSM for single-site saturation mutagenesis, MSDM (called QCLM in the current code/UI/API) for multisite combinatorial mutagenesis, and PAS for mutagenic or de novo gene synthesis. These are oligo/primer design workflows for experimentally building libraries; they are not three names for one generic enumerator.
- **Entry/interface:** web scenes; `POST /v1/ssm`, `POST /v1/qclm`, and `POST /v1/pas`; Celery tasks `tasks.ssm`, `tasks.qclm`, `tasks.pas`; internal `ssm_solve`, `qclm_solve`, `pas_solve` ([server routes, lines 53–65](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/api/server_fastapi.py#L53-L65), [tasks.py, lines 42–73](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/tasks.py#L42-L73)).
- **Documentation status:** all three are first-class documented workflows in the paper; the HTTP routes are current documented application API surfaces, while direct Python calls are internal/source-level rather than a promised library API.
- **Restrictions:** outputs are primers or synthesis oligos and mixture instructions. The program does **not** output an explicit collection of every full-length DNA or protein library member; combinatorial diversity is encoded physically in primers/oligos/degenerate codons.
- **Evidence:** paper Figure 1 and Results sections “Single Site Saturation Mutagenesis,” “Multi Site Directed Mutagenesis,” and “PCR-based Accurate Synthesis”; [README, lines 1–8 and 285–287](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/README.md#L1-L8).

#### 2. SSSM: parallel single-site saturation-mutagenesis primer pairs

- **What it does:** for each requested amino-acid site, designs an overlapping forward/reverse mutagenic primer pair. Each resulting construct is intended to contain one saturated site; products can be pooled after parallel overlap-extension/SOEing reactions to make a single-site saturation library.
- **Entry/interface:** SSM web form and results view; `POST /v1/ssm`; internal `SSMInput` and `ssm_solve`.
- **Documentation status:** first-class paper/UI/API workflow.
- **Restrictions:** the user supplies a plasmid sequence, gene of interest, forward/reverse flanking primers, mutation positions, and degenerate codon(s). This is not simultaneous multisite mutation in each SSM clone; that is the role of QCLM/PAS.
- **Evidence:** paper Figure 1a and SSSM Results (journal pp. 358–360); [`SSMInput`, lines 211–218](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/ssm_types.py#L211-L218).

#### 3. SSSM has fast and exhaustive-like search engines, with optional Primer3

- **What it does:** offers a fast approximation that grows primers from the overlap, and a slower precompute/filter (“brute force”) search. The latter may use Primer3 as its candidate generator; Primer3 jobs can be parallelized with the supplied Lambda service.
- **Entry/interface:** SSM form toggles; `SSMConfig.use_fast_approximation_algorithm` and `.use_primer3`; Lambda deployment/environment settings; internal SSM solver/generator classes.
- **Documentation status:** algorithms and Primer3 option are documented in the paper and README; deployment details are documented source-application configuration, not a separate design API.
- **Restrictions:** fast mode is faster but need not find the best-scoring design. The “brute force” algorithm is not globally exhaustive across all possible primers: the paper says it searches candidates in sliding melting-temperature windows. Primer3 is optional and is only used in the non-fast path; the UI makes fast approximation and Primer3 mutually exclusive. Lambda is disabled by default and requires local Docker/SAM or AWS setup and credentials.
- **Evidence:** paper SSSM algorithm and Discussion (journal pp. 359–360, 364–365); [SSM config, lines 132–208](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/ssm_types.py#L132-L208); [README Lambda settings, lines 226–282](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/README.md#L226-L282).

#### 4. SSSM exposes unusually detailed primer constraints and soft-objective scoring

- **What it does:** controls primer, mutation-adjacent 3′ segment, 5′ segment and overlap lengths; total and segment melting temperatures; GC content; GC clamp; maximum forward/reverse Tm difference; hairpin and homo/heterodimer checks; and weights used to rank competing solutions. It reports primer properties, scores and constraint violations.
- **Entry/interface:** SSM form and JSON `SSMConfig`; lower-level temperature/secondary-structure calculators.
- **Documentation status:** the main constraints/scoring are documented in the paper/UI/API data model. A few source fields, including candidate-calculation method, maximum combinations, fully custom `TemperatureConfig`, and separate forward/reverse temperature configurations, are source/API-only rather than exposed by the normal web form.
- **Restrictions:** primer and 3′ length bounds are hard constraints; the paper characterizes most other preferences as soft constraints. Scoring constants are empirical and were not exhaustively experimentally optimized. The paper explicitly says GC-clamp optimization was not implemented, even though a current `gc_clamp` configuration field exists; a field must not be read as proof of the claimed optimization.
- **Evidence:** paper SSSM Methods/Discussion (journal pp. 359–360, 364–365); [`SSMConfig`, lines 132–208](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/ssm_types.py#L132-L208).

#### 5. SSSM accepts amino-acid mutation syntax and custom IUPAC degenerate codons

- **What it does:** accepts mutation identifiers such as `[wild-type amino acid][1-based position]X` (and the parser can also represent a specific target amino acid), inserts a chosen IUPAC degenerate codon at each target, and supports comma-separated alternative degenerate codons.
- **Entry/interface:** SSM web input; `SSMInput.mutations` and `.degenerate_codon`; internal `AminoMutation` parser.
- **Documentation status:** mutation/codon input is first-class. The ability of the underlying mutation parser to accept a non-`X` target is source-level and does not turn SSM into the normal targeted-mutation workflow.
- **Restrictions:** the backend data-model default is `NNS`, whereas the paper and current UI use `NNK` as the normal default. Multiple comma-separated codons are alternatives, not a single compound codon.
- **Evidence:** paper SSSM input discussion; [`SSMInput`, lines 211–218](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/ssm_types.py#L211-L218); [`AminoMutation`, lines 28–71](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/mutation.py#L28-L71).

#### 6. SSSM can derive a compact degenerate-codon set from desired/forbidden amino acids

- **What it does:** the web client can calculate one or more IUPAC codons to include selected amino acids while avoiding selected amino acids, or the user can enter codons directly. This is a small codon-cover design subfeature in addition to the default NNK input.
- **Entry/interface:** SSM web component `aminoAcidCodons.tsx`, with the combinatorial search implemented client-side in `amino.ts`; the result is submitted as the normal SSM `degenerate_codon` string.
- **Documentation status:** described in the paper and present in UI code, but **not a separate backend/API endpoint or documented Python API**.
- **Restrictions:** stop codons are avoided by default; amino acids not requested and not explicitly forbidden may still be encoded. The implementation caps recursive combinations at 100 and gives the search a ten-minute deadline, so it is heuristic/bounded rather than a guaranteed global minimum.
- **Evidence:** paper SSSM degenerate-codon paragraph (journal p. 360); [`amino.ts`, lines 23–24 and 197–277](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/frontend/src/scenes/SSM/components/amino.ts#L23-L24).

#### 7. SSSM handles circular plasmid context and flanking-primer presets/customization

- **What it does:** locates the gene and supplied flanking primers in a circular plasmid, including wrap-around extraction, to construct the sequence context used for primer design. The UI offers preset pET SeqF1/SeqR1 flanks or custom primer sequences.
- **Entry/interface:** SSM web form; `SSMSequences.get_full_sequence_with_offset` and related source methods.
- **Documentation status:** plasmid/GOI/flanking inputs are first-class paper/UI inputs; wrap-around and uniqueness checks are implementation details.
- **Restrictions:** the gene, forward primer and reverse-primer reverse complement must each occur exactly once in the plasmid or validation fails. UI custom flanking primers are capped at 60 nt. A dormant direct-flanking-sequence branch in source is marked TODO and invokes absent helpers, so it should not be counted as a dependable extra mode.
- **Evidence:** [`ssm_types.py`, lines 35–84](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/ssm_types.py#L35-L84); [`SSMForm.tsx`, lines 160–297](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/frontend/src/scenes/SSM/components/SSMForm.tsx#L160-L297).

#### 8. SSSM produces ordering-ready, plate-oriented primer exports

- **What it does:** displays one primer-pair design per target and exports separate forward/reverse XLSX worksheets with well position, oligo name, sequence and notes. Each comma-separated alternative degenerate codon receives its own pair of worksheets and substituted primer sequences.
- **Entry/interface:** result page/`SaveFile` client component; server `GET /v1/export_ssm/{task_id}.xlsx` also exists.
- **Documentation status:** spreadsheet/output functionality is documented; exact 96-well layout behavior is implementation-only.
- **Restrictions:** the on-screen backend result substitutes only the first codon, while the client XLSX materializes all alternatives. The current client creates “Second” worksheets only at result index 96 and has no third-plate transition; designs beyond 192 entries repeat second-sheet well coordinates, an implementation ceiling/defect rather than a documented promise.
- **Evidence:** [`SaveFile`, lines 35 and 92–149](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/frontend/src/shared/components/SaveFile/index.tsx#L35-L35); [`ssm.py`, lines 775–803](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/ssm.py#L775-L803); [server export, lines 134–145](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/api/server_fastapi.py#L134-L145).

#### 9. QCLM/MSDM designs primers for multisite combinatorial libraries

- **What it does:** designs unidirectional primers for the QuikChange Lightning Multi protocol to introduce user-specified amino-acid substitutions at multiple sites and thereby construct combinatorial variant libraries.
- **Entry/interface:** QCLM/MSDM web scene; `POST /v1/qclm`; internal `QCLMInput` and `qclm_solve`.
- **Documentation status:** first-class documented workflow. The paper calls it MSDM; the current implementation calls it QCLM.
- **Restrictions:** input is DNA gene sequence with optional 5′/3′ flanks and amino-acid mutation strings. It is a primer-design workflow, not a full-length variant sequence enumerator.
- **Evidence:** paper Figure 1b and MSDM Results (journal pp. 360–362); [`qclm_types.py`, lines 83–107](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/qclm_types.py#L83-L107).

#### 10. QCLM groups substitutions by site and can span several nearby sites with one primer

- **What it does:** parses multiple target substitutions at the same position into one site, automatically includes wild type at each site, partitions nearby sites into primer-coverable groups, and can produce a single primer containing codons for multiple mutation sites. It generates the codon/primer combinations needed for those groups.
- **Entry/interface:** normal QCLM workflow; internal `QCLMInput.parse_mutations`, mutation grouping, and QCLM solver.
- **Documentation status:** multisite/grouping behavior is documented in the paper; automatic wild-type inclusion and precise grouping mechanics are source details.
- **Restrictions:** which sites can share a primer is limited by length/Tm constraints and positional separation. The output represents combinations through a set of primers/degenerate codons, not complete assembled genes.
- **Evidence:** paper MSDM algorithm (journal pp. 360–362); [`QCLMInput.parse_mutations`, lines 96–107](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/qclm_types.py#L96-L107); [mutation grouping, lines 119–177](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/mutation.py#L119-L177).

#### 11. QCLM supports degenerate or separate codons and organism-aware rare-codon filtering

- **What it does:** can compress desired amino-acid alternatives into IUPAC degenerate codons, or disable degeneracy and make separate nondegenerate codon choices. Codons can be selected using an organism usage table with a minimum-frequency threshold, and stop-generating degenerate codons are rejected.
- **Entry/interface:** QCLM form/config; codon-usage selector; internal QCLM codon/set-cover routines.
- **Documentation status:** organism selection, cutoff, and degenerate-codon option are first-class paper/UI capabilities; the fallback and two current wiring limitations below are source-level findings.
- **Restrictions:** the degenerate set-cover search has a one-minute limit and falls back to random nondegenerate codons when no result is found. Current source has two important species-specificity limitations: (1) the frontend submits `codon_usage`/`taxonomy_id`, while backend `QCLMConfig` and the solver read `organism`, leaving no mapped current-UI path that populates the selected organism; (2) QCLM constructs the degeneracy solver without passing the selected organism, whose own default is E. coli. Thus the current source does not reliably implement the paper’s end-to-end custom-species claim for QCLM degenerate-codon selection.
- **Evidence:** paper MSDM codon-usage/degeneracy discussion (journal pp. 361–362); [`qclm.py`, lines 267–342](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/qclm.py#L267-L342); [`QCLMConfig`, lines 28–80](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/qclm_types.py#L28-L80); [frontend request mapping, lines 145–175](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/frontend/src/services/api.ts#L145-L175); [`Degeneracy` default, lines 173–186](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/pas_degeneracy_recursion.py#L173-L186).

#### 12. QCLM can enforce nonoverlapping primers, or allow overlapping primers for staged rounds

- **What it does:** can require candidate primers not to overlap so all can anneal in one reaction; if overlap is allowed, it marks primers that overlap the following primer, supporting successive mutagenesis rounds.
- **Entry/interface:** QCLM `non_overlapping_primers` form/config option and `overlap_with_following` result field.
- **Documentation status:** first-class paper/UI/API behavior.
- **Restrictions:** the nonoverlap requirement narrows feasible solutions; allowing overlaps changes the experimental protocol rather than resolving simultaneous annealing.
- **Evidence:** paper MSDM discussion (journal p. 361); [`qclm_types.py`, lines 52–56](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/qclm_types.py#L52-L56); [`qclm.py`, lines 358–369](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/qclm.py#L358-L369).

#### 13. QCLM optimizes primer feasibility, uniformity, structure, and mutation coverage—but may omit sites

- **What it does:** scores length, GC, Tm, hairpin/dimer risk, Tm uniformity, and mutation-site coverage; the coverage weight lets the solver trade coverage against other objectives. Primer and 5′/3′ length bounds are hard, while other targets are generally soft.
- **Entry/interface:** QCLM web configuration/JSON `QCLMConfig`; normal result records contain sequence, Tm, GC, mutations, degenerate codons and overlap status.
- **Documentation status:** first-class documented scoring capability and restriction.
- **Restrictions:** the best-scoring solution can cover only a subset of requested mutation sites. Current solver logs a warning for incomplete coverage, but the JSON output has no global coverage/warning field, so a caller must compare requested and returned mutations.
- **Evidence:** paper MSDM algorithm/limitations (journal pp. 361–362, 364); [`QCLMConfig`, lines 28–80](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/qclm_types.py#L28-L80); [`QCLMForm.tsx`, lines 125–319](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/frontend/src/scenes/QCLM/components/QCLMForm.tsx#L125-L319).

#### 14. PAS designs overlapping oligo fragments for ordinary or mutagenic de novo gene synthesis

- **What it does:** partitions an input gene into an even number of alternating-strand overlapping oligos for PCR-based Accurate Synthesis. It can design a wild-type synthesis tiling or incorporate specified mutations, including combinatorial mixtures within fragments.
- **Entry/interface:** PAS web scene; `POST /v1/pas`; internal `PASInput` and `pas_solve`.
- **Documentation status:** first-class paper/UI/API workflow; zero-mutation operation is supported by the backend/UI logic but is less prominently explained than mutagenic synthesis.
- **Restrictions:** output is fragment oligos, not a ready-made list of every assembled full-gene variant. Mutation sites are kept out of overlap regions. The algorithm seeks an even fragment count with alternating orientation.
- **Evidence:** paper Figure 1c and PAS Results (journal pp. 362–364); [`pas.py`, fragment search](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/pas.py); [`pas_output.py`, output formatter](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/pas_output.py).

#### 15. PAS accepts DNA or protein and performs host-biased reverse translation

- **What it does:** accepts a DNA gene directly or reverse-translates a protein into a DNA coding sequence. Reverse translation probabilistically samples codons using host codon frequencies, a rare-codon cutoff, GC objectives and avoided sequence motifs, retaining higher-scoring candidates.
- **Entry/interface:** PAS sequence-type control and codon-usage configuration; internal `Translator`.
- **Documentation status:** first-class documented PAS capability; sampling/termination constants are source details.
- **Restrictions:** reverse translation is stochastic/heuristic, not guaranteed globally optimal. The current `Translator` stops after 600 consecutive nonimproving candidates and has a hard ten-minute timeout. The paper notes that whole-transcript RNA secondary structure is not assessed.
- **Evidence:** paper PAS algorithm/Discussion (journal pp. 362–365); [`reverse_translation.py`, lines 29–105](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/reverse_translation.py#L29-L105).

#### 16. PAS accepts targeted amino-acid or DNA-codon mutations with desired frequencies

- **What it does:** mutation rows specify a 1-based residue site, one or more desired amino acids or explicit codons, and target frequency percentages. For each site it adds the wild-type choice when mutant frequencies sum to less than one.
- **Entry/interface:** PAS mutation table; `PASMutationSite`/`PASMutation`; optional spreadsheet upload.
- **Documentation status:** first-class documented input. Spreadsheet ingestion is UI/example implementation rather than a backend endpoint.
- **Restrictions:** frequencies at a site must be coherent; wild-type fraction is calculated as `1 − sum(mutant frequencies)`. Protein-letter and explicit-DNA-codon input are separate modes.
- **Evidence:** paper PAS input description (journal p. 363); [`pas_types.py`](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/pas_types.py); [`pas_output.py`, lines 164–242](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/pas_output.py#L164-L242).

#### 17. PAS materializes the Cartesian product of mutation choices within each fragment and calculates mixing ratios

- **What it does:** groups mutations carried by the same fragment, generates an oligo for each cross-site combination, and reports each oligo’s mixing ratio as the normalized product of the independent per-site probabilities. It reports wild-type and mutated codons/amino acids for interpreting the mixture.
- **Entry/interface:** normal PAS output; internal `OligoGenerator` and output formatter; XLSX export includes mutation syntax, mix ratio and target MT%.
- **Documentation status:** combinatorial-oligo/mixing behavior is described in the paper and visible in output; exact Cartesian-product and normalization implementation is source-level.
- **Restrictions:** combinations across different fragments are not emitted as explicit full-length sequences. The multiplication assumes independent target-site frequencies. The current output schema requires a numeric `mix_ratio`; the paper’s broader warning that a degenerate codon can make precise ratios unknown should not be uncritically projected onto every current output.
- **Evidence:** [`generate_oligos.py`, lines 122–142 and 238–309](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/generate_oligos.py#L122-L142); [`SaveFile`, lines 198–254](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/frontend/src/shared/components/SaveFile/index.tsx#L198-L254); paper PAS output discussion (journal pp. 363–364).

#### 18. PAS can compress equal-frequency alternatives into degenerate codons

- **What it does:** optionally seeks a degenerate codon covering desired alternatives, reducing the number of distinct oligos needed for a fragment.
- **Entry/interface:** PAS “use degenerate codons” configuration; internal degeneracy recursion used by the oligo generator.
- **Documentation status:** first-class paper/UI/config option.
- **Restrictions:** current implementation only merges alternatives having equal requested probabilities; degenerate codons can encode unintended amino acids and can make exact ratios infeasible in the more general method described by the paper. Degeneracy is off by default in the backend config.
- **Evidence:** paper PAS degeneracy discussion (journal pp. 363–364); [`PASConfig`, lines 29–69](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/pas_types.py#L29-L69); [`generate_oligos.py`](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/generate_oligos.py).

#### 19. PAS optimizes fragment geometry and overlap uniformity with a bounded search

- **What it does:** searches for a low-scoring partition under min/opt/max fragment length, overlap length, overlap Tm and GC bounds; groups nearby mutation sites on fragments while excluding them from overlaps; penalizes nonuniform fragment lengths/Tms and structural issues.
- **Entry/interface:** PAS form/`PASConfig`; internal backtracking/dynamic-programming fragment search.
- **Documentation status:** first-class paper/UI/config behavior; exact search deadline is source-only.
- **Restrictions:** the optimizer is heuristic and uses a hardcoded five-second search allowance for each overlap-temperature attempt, so it may fail to find a global optimum. The paper calls out the hardcoded timeout and simplified fragment scoring. Backend defaults (e.g. 40/56/90 nt fragments) differ from current web-form defaults (40/40/60), so defaults must be qualified by interface.
- **Evidence:** paper PAS algorithm/Discussion (journal pp. 363–365); [`pas.py`, lines 32–33 and 232–301](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/pas.py#L32-L33); [`PASConfig`, lines 29–69](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/pas_types.py#L29-L69).

#### 20. PAS supports a large restriction-site/motif avoidance list plus custom IUPAC motifs

- **What it does:** lets the user select named restriction-enzyme recognition motifs (the current UI resource contains 162 entries) and enter arbitrary IUPAC DNA motifs to avoid during protein reverse translation and oligo design.
- **Entry/interface:** PAS avoided-motif selector/config; internal motif compiler and reverse-translation/oligo scoring.
- **Documentation status:** motif avoidance is first-class paper/UI functionality; the exact catalog and implementation defect below are source-level.
- **Restrictions:** motif avoidance participates in a heuristic search and is not a formal guarantee. More specifically, the current mutated-oligo filter tests the original DNA string rather than each generated mutated oligo, so mutations that newly create an avoided motif are not reliably excluded.
- **Evidence:** paper PAS input/Discussion; [`motifs.py`, lines 21–52](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/motifs.py#L21-L52); [motif catalog](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/frontend/src/shared/motifs.json); [`generate_oligos.py`](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/generate_oligos.py).

#### 21. Cross-workflow Tm and secondary-structure machinery is configurable beyond the web defaults

- **What it does:** supports Wallace, GC-content, nearest-neighbor and NEB-like melting-temperature calculations, multiple thermodynamic tables/salt corrections, and configurable DNA, Na, K, Tris, Mg and dNTP concentrations. Primer/oligo candidates can be evaluated for hairpins and homo/heterodimers as appropriate to the protocol.
- **Entry/interface:** nested JSON/source `TemperatureConfig` and its `create_calculator`; SSM/QCLM/PAS web forms expose only a subset. SSM/QCLM offer structure-check controls; PAS scores fragment hairpin/homodimer regions, while heterodimers are intentionally not treated as defects where PAS overlaps are supposed to hybridize.
- **Documentation status:** basic Tm/structure checks are documented; calculation method/table/chemistry choices are **source/API-only**, not a standalone documented end-user interface.
- **Restrictions:** these are supporting design calculations, not a separate sequence-library generator. The current PAS frontend sends a `compute_hairpin_homodimer` checkbox value, but `PASConfig` has no corresponding boolean and the PAS solver performs its own structure mapping, so that checkbox is not a dependable backend on/off control. The paper also says PAS evaluates fragment-level structures, not secondary structure over the entire reverse-translated sequence.
- **Evidence:** [`TemperatureConfig`, lines 238–299](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/temperature_calculator.py#L238-L299); [`PASConfig`, lines 29–69](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/pas_types.py#L29-L69); paper Methods and Discussion (journal pp. 364–365).

#### 22. Species/codon-usage lookup is a public supporting interface

- **What it does:** retrieves organism names/taxonomy IDs and codon-frequency data used for codon selection; the paper reports 33 genetic codes and 35,799 species in its source database, while the current checked-in species table has 35,793 rows. E. coli and yeast are convenient presets, with a broader custom-species selector.

- **Restrictions:** database counts differ between the published paper and current checkout and must be dated. QCLM’s current organism-wiring limitations are recorded in capability 11; PAS does pass an `organism` setting through its current request mapping.

- **What it does:** presents result tables plus an adapted sequence feature viewer with primer-direction arrows, mutation alignment/highlighting, a GC graph and synchronized table/sequence selection. The browser can print the results, and the client writes XLSX files for all three workflows: plate-formatted SSM pairs, QCLM primer ordering rows, and PAS colored fragment/mutation/mix-ratio rows.

#### 25. Workflows run as asynchronous jobs with lifecycle controls

- **What it does:** submits designs to Celery/Redis workers and returns URLs to check status, fetch results, cancel a job, forget its stored result, or export it. This permits long-running library designs without holding one HTTP request open.
- **Entry/interface:** workflow POST routes and `GET /v1/check/{id}`, `/result/{id}`, `/cancel/{id}`, `/forget/{id}`; Celery tasks.
- **Documentation status:** the distributed architecture is documented in the paper/README; lifecycle routes are current API details.
- **Restrictions:** this is job orchestration, not an additional design algorithm. The result endpoint returns a traceback for failed tasks, and the public request model is unstructured `Any`, so clients must learn the real schemas from frontend/source examples.
- **Evidence:** [server, lines 43–117](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/api/server_fastapi.py#L43-L117); [README, lines 46–66 and 207–218](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/README.md#L46-L66); paper Implementation/Methods.

#### 26. Lower-level codon and degenerate-codon operations exist, but are not a fourth public workflow

- **What it does:** source modules can translate/decode IUPAC triplets, enumerate codon representations, solve set-cover-like codon choices, read usage tables, and calculate mutation/codon combinations. These utilities enable SSM/QCLM/PAS and could be imported by a developer.
- **Entry/interface:** internal modules such as `degenerate_codon.py`, `pas_degeneracy_recursion.py`, `mutation.py`, `codon_usage_table.py`, and `usage_table.py`.
- **Documentation status:** implementation/source-only; neither a documented stable Python API nor a CLI/subcommand.
- **Restrictions:** there is no installed package, semantic-versioned library contract, or standalone HTTP endpoint for arbitrary codon-set design. Counting these helpers as separate end-user capabilities would misattribute internal implementation details to the public product.
- **Evidence:** [backend source tree](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/tree/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker); [README architecture, lines 207–223](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/README.md#L207-L223).

### Global restrictions that qualify all capability claims

- Mutation Maker is an **oligo/primer and mixture designer**. It makes physical library-construction plans but does not enumerate or return a FASTA collection of complete variant DNA/protein sequences.
- SSSM, QCLM and PAS are optimization heuristics with hard limits, soft objectives, empirical weights and/or timeouts. “Optimizes” does not mean “proves a global optimum.” The paper expressly identifies the sliding-window SSSM search, possible QCLM mutation undercoverage, PAS timeouts/simplified scoring, absence of GC-clamp optimization, and lack of full reverse-transcript structure analysis.
- Current behavior and the 2021 paper are not identical. The current app has post-paper framework/dependency changes and several source/UI wiring discrepancies; conversely, the paper describes temporary result sharing and PDF output that are not present in current source. Claims must be explicitly dated or qualified when those versions differ.
- There is no packaged CLI or published PyPI/npm release. The supported product surfaces are a self-hosted web application and asynchronous HTTP service; direct Python calls are internal/source-level.

This is material because enabling degenerate codons does not guarantee a compressed degenerate-primer result.

8. **PAS’s exact physical tiling mode.** The record calls PAS de novo synthesis and quotes an even-fragment constraint, but does not explain that it produces an even number of alternating forward/reverse-complement oligos and deliberately keeps mutation codons out of overlaps ([`pas.py`](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/pas.py), [`PASOligoOutput.make_reverse_complement`, lines 168–183](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/pas_types.py#L168-L183), paper PAS Results). This is material because it identifies the actual synthesis architecture, not merely that generic “fragments” are returned.

9. **PAS can run with no requested mutations.** Its solver explicitly handles an empty mutation list and can produce a wild-type/codon-optimized de novo oligo tiling ([`pas.py`, lines 186–203](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/pas.py#L186-L203)). This is material because PAS is not limited to mutagenic libraries; it is also an ordinary gene-synthesis design mode.

10. **PAS degeneracy is limited to equal-probability alternatives.** Current PAS only merges amino-acid alternatives into a degenerate codon when their requested probabilities match; otherwise it leaves separate codons/oligos ([`generate_oligos.py`, lines 238–307](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/generate_oligos.py#L238-L307)). This is material because the record credits degenerate compression and frequency control without stating the condition under which both can coexist.

11. **PAS custom motifs and the current motif-check defect.** Beyond the named restriction-enzyme catalog, the UI accepts arbitrary IUPAC DNA motifs; however, the generated-oligo acceptance test searches the original `dna` rather than each candidate oligo, so a mutation-created forbidden motif is not reliably rejected ([`motifs.py`, lines 21–52](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/motifs.py#L21-L52), [`generate_oligos.py`, lines 311–328](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/generate_oligos.py#L311-L328)). This is material because motif avoidance is broader than the named-enzyme list but weaker than the record’s unqualified synthesis-constraint description suggests.

12. **PAS’s structure-check checkbox is not a backend switch.** The client sends `compute_hairpin_homodimer`, but `PASConfig` declares no such property; PAS instead retains an always-present `hairpin_homodimer_weight` and performs its own fragment structure scoring ([frontend mapping, lines 275–303](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/frontend/src/services/api.ts#L275-L303), [`PASConfig`, lines 29–69](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/pas_types.py#L29-L69)). This is material because the visible control does not actually enable/disable the calculation as a user would expect.

13. **Important current solver bounds are missing.** In addition to the general heuristic warnings the record quotes, current PAS reverse translation has a ten-minute hard timeout and a 600-nonimprovement stopping rule, while fragment search uses five seconds per Tm attempt ([`reverse_translation.py`, lines 29–105](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/reverse_translation.py#L29-L105), [`pas.py`, lines 32–33](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/pas.py#L32-L33)). These are material because they turn an abstract “may be nonoptimal” caveat into concrete scalability and reproducibility limits.

14. **Web-form defaults and backend defaults are not the same.** For example, backend PAS defaults are 40/56/90 nt for min/opt/max oligo length, whereas the current form defaults are 40/40/60; other overlap defaults also differ ([`PASConfig`, lines 29–69](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/pas_types.py#L29-L69), [PAS form](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/frontend/src/scenes/PAS/components/PASForm/index.tsx)). This is material because “default settings” produce different designs depending on whether the caller uses the web mapping or constructs backend JSON directly.

15. **The current FastAPI documentation does not describe workflow schemas.** FastAPI supplies `/docs`, but all workflow routes accept one `TaskBody` whose `data` is typed `Any`; the structured SSM/QCLM/PAS contracts remain discoverable only from frontend/source examples ([`server_fastapi.py`, lines 25–65](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/api/server_fastapi.py#L25-L65)). This is material to understanding how usable the claimed REST interface is without the GUI.

### C. BALANCE

The record is capability-heavy rather than limitation-heavy. It gives unusually extensive space and concrete source detail to all three workflows, PAS’s fragment-level enumeration and ratios, codon/degenerate-codon design, thermodynamics, visualization, validation, input formats, and deployment; the tool’s genuine strengths are given at least proportionate space. It also contains a substantial limitations section, but several consequential current-code restrictions and UI/backend wiring defects are absent, while paper-only PDF/share features and an inert GC-clamp field are credited as current capabilities. Overall it represents Mutation Maker generously, not dismissively: breadth is fair, but present-day capability/readiness is modestly overstated.
