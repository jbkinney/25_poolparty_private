# Row 19 audit — synthesis-constraint checking

Audited 2026-08-17 against the current row definition and the pinned primary-source revisions listed below. No pre-existing score or comparison was used as evidence.

## Binding operational test

I applied the global rule literally: credit requires a tool-provided operation, parameter, or mode, not a capability reconstructed from unrelated primitives. This row counts **detection** of physical synthesis/cloning problems in designed library sequences—checking, reporting, rejection, or filtering—including matching against an external background. It excludes biological-content checks and primer production.

For a consistent interpretation of the row's quantity words:

- `yes`: at least three distinct physical constraint classes are exposed as named, documented checks;
- `partial`: one or two such named classes, or only a tool-provided generic predicate/check mechanism;
- `no`: no qualifying check; a metric alone is not a thresholded check, and primer-only scoring is reserved for row 20;
- `unknown`: only if the relevant public surface is genuinely indeterminable.

The row-11 boundary is also binding: changing a sequence to satisfy a condition is not itself checking. A repair mechanism counts here only when the tool independently exposes the check or its violation. The row-20 boundary prevents crediting primer design and its internal primer-selection penalties as library-sequence checking.

For disconfirmation, I searched each complete checked-out public tree (source, API/docs, tests/examples) for the plausible surface: `constraint`, `synthesis`, `length`, `GC`, `homopolymer`, `repeat`, `restriction`, `digest`, `hairpin`, `dimer`, `melting`, `background`, `off-target`, `filter`, `reject`, and `violation`, then inspected semantic hits rather than treating grep matches as capability evidence.

## Results

| Tool | Value | Confidence | Operational finding |
|---|---|---:|---|
| PoolParty | `yes` | high | Named filters cover GC range, homopolymers, repeat/low complexity, and restriction sites, and rejected sequences can be removed from generated output. |
| VaLiAnT | `partial` | high | It provides and reports a min/max oligo-length filter, but no second physical constraint class was found. |
| MPRAnator | `partial` | high | It filters candidate barcodes by GC and reports duplicate restriction sites in final oligos; no broader physical QC surface was found. |
| MPRA Design Tools (`mpradesigntools` / `designMPRA`) | `partial` | high | It checks aberrant restriction sites and handles a maximum construct length, with explicit failures/notes, but does not expose several distinct physical-check classes. |
| Oligopool Calculator (`oligopool`) | `yes` | high | A dedicated `verify` QC operation reports per-oligo length, excluded-motif, and external-background conflicts with details and aggregate statistics. |
| Mutation Maker | `no` | high | Hairpin/dimer, GC, and Tm calculations are confined to primer-producing workflows; there is no separate library-sequence checking operation. |
| DNA Chisel | `yes` | high | Numerous named physical specifications can be evaluated independently, and a standalone breaches-report API checks collections without repairing them. |
| tangermeme | `no` | high | It exposes a GC metric and generic model/loss-driven design, but no physical constraint predicate, violation report, or synthesis/cloning filter. |

## Per-tool evidence and reasoning

### 1. PoolParty — `yes` (high confidence)

**Primary evidence.** Audited the canonical supplied `jbkinney/poolparty-statetracker` snapshot at commit [`1bb0179`](https://github.com/jbkinney/poolparty-statetracker/tree/1bb0179e1c3720b1fffd471802b3040f9336de28) (`v0.1.1-12-g1bb0179`). `FilterMixin` states that its filters replace rejected sequences with `NullSeq` and that `generate_library(discard_null_seqs=True)` removes them ([filter_mixin.py lines 12–18](https://github.com/jbkinney/poolparty-statetracker/blob/1bb0179e1c3720b1fffd471802b3040f9336de28/poolparty/src/poolparty/pool_mixins/filter_mixin.py#L12-L18)). It then documents and implements:

- `filter_gc(min_gc, max_gc)`, returning `NullSeq` outside the range ([lines 20–63](https://github.com/jbkinney/poolparty-statetracker/blob/1bb0179e1c3720b1fffd471802b3040f9336de28/poolparty/src/poolparty/pool_mixins/filter_mixin.py#L20-L63));
- `filter_homopolymer(max_length)`, explicitly motivated by synthesis problems and filtering overlong runs ([lines 65–104](https://github.com/jbkinney/poolparty-statetracker/blob/1bb0179e1c3720b1fffd471802b3040f9336de28/poolparty/src/poolparty/pool_mixins/filter_mixin.py#L65-L104));
- linguistic-complexity and DUST filters for repetitive/low-complexity sequences ([lines 106–192](https://github.com/jbkinney/poolparty-statetracker/blob/1bb0179e1c3720b1fffd471802b3040f9336de28/poolparty/src/poolparty/pool_mixins/filter_mixin.py#L106-L192));
- `filter_restriction_sites`, with enzyme presets, custom IUPAC sites, reverse-complement checking, and cloning-strategy motivation ([lines 194–281](https://github.com/jbkinney/poolparty-statetracker/blob/1bb0179e1c3720b1fffd471802b3040f9336de28/poolparty/src/poolparty/pool_mixins/filter_mixin.py#L194-L281)).

The underlying public `filter(predicate)` additionally records a `passed` design-card key and documents output exclusion ([common_ops_mixin.py lines 212–245](https://github.com/jbkinney/poolparty-statetracker/blob/1bb0179e1c3720b1fffd471802b3040f9336de28/poolparty/src/poolparty/pool_mixins/common_ops_mixin.py#L212-L245)), but the `yes` does not depend on that generic hook: four named physical classes already meet the threshold.

**Reasoning.** These are rejection/filter operations, not sequence repair. They therefore score row 19 directly and do not earn row-11 credit for the same mechanisms.

**Disconfirmation attempt.** I tried to reduce this to a generic user-authored predicate or mere utilities. That fails: the four named methods supply the thresholds, documented physical meanings, and filter behavior themselves. What would change the score: if these methods were only examples around `filter(predicate)` rather than shipped `DnaPool` methods, the result would fall to `partial`; the versioned source shows otherwise.

### 2. VaLiAnT — `partial` (high confidence)

**Primary evidence.** Audited `cancerit/VaLiAnT` at commit [`8796cc1`](https://github.com/cancerit/VaLiAnT/tree/8796cc112dafd4919fec59913f58cd2be87c45eb) (`4.0.0-1-g8796cc1`). The README says oligos above `max-length` are omitted from the unique-oligo output and written to a separate `excluded` metadata file ([README lines 110–120](https://github.com/cancerit/VaLiAnT/blob/8796cc112dafd4919fec59913f58cd2be87c45eb/README.md#L110-L120)); the option table names the maximum oligo length ([lines 219–225](https://github.com/cancerit/VaLiAnT/blob/8796cc112dafd4919fec59913f58cd2be87c45eb/README.md#L219-L225)). The CLI exposes both minimum and maximum length and logs how many too-short and too-long oligos were discarded ([common_cli.py lines 56–98](https://github.com/cancerit/VaLiAnT/blob/8796cc112dafd4919fec59913f58cd2be87c45eb/src/valiant/common_cli.py#L56-L98)). `OligoGenerationInfo.eval_in_range` performs the threshold test and increments separate counters ([oligo_generation_info.py lines 26–65](https://github.com/cancerit/VaLiAnT/blob/8796cc112dafd4919fec59913f58cd2be87c45eb/src/valiant/oligo_generation_info.py#L26-L65)); the metadata writer sends failures to the excluded file while only in-range sequences enter the unique set ([meta_table.py lines 585–632](https://github.com/cancerit/VaLiAnT/blob/8796cc112dafd4919fec59913f58cd2be87c45eb/src/valiant/meta_table.py#L585-L632)).

**Reasoning.** This is a named, documented, reporting-and-filtering operation for one physical class (synthesis length), hence `partial`. Background VCF application and its `bg-mask` are biological-variant handling, not sequence matching/cross-hybridisation against an external reference, so they do not add a second row-19 class.

**Disconfirmation attempt.** I searched all package modules, README, tests, and examples for GC, repeat/homopolymer, restriction/digest, structure, Tm, homology/off-target, and synthesis-provider checks. The only qualifying implemented surface was oligo length. The cDNA example's external digest assets/workflow do not become a VaLiAnT check. What would change the score: another named physical checker, or a generic sequence predicate exposed by VaLiAnT, would preserve/raise credit; three distinct named classes would support `yes`.

### 3. MPRAnator — `partial` (high confidence)

**Primary evidence.** Audited `hemberg-lab/MPRAnator` at commit [`9969790`](https://github.com/hemberg-lab/MPRAnator/tree/9969790d62410138d4281b7955da6d085f07b1bc). `barcode_generator` rejects/generated-again candidates until their GC lies between the requested minimum and maximum, while also attempting barcode separation ([part1.py lines 37–88](https://github.com/hemberg-lab/MPRAnator/blob/9969790d62410138d4281b7955da6d085f07b1bc/part1.py#L37-L88)). After assembling each final oligo, `createMPRAResultOutput` searches the complete sequence for extra occurrences of each requested restriction site and appends `DUPLICATE_RESTRICTION_SITES` to that oligo's FASTA header ([part1.py lines 210–291](https://github.com/hemberg-lab/MPRAnator/blob/9969790d62410138d4281b7955da6d085f07b1bc/part1.py#L210-L291)). The application's own documentation defines that header field as reporting a restriction site with multiple copies in the oligo ([views.py lines 232–243](https://github.com/hemberg-lab/MPRAnator/blob/9969790d62410138d4281b7955da6d085f07b1bc/iliasApp/views.py#L232-L243)).

**Reasoning.** GC filtering of candidate barcode sequences and per-oligo restriction-site reporting are two tool-provided physical checks, which is the row's `partial` case. The 20,000-base form limit is an application input-size guard, not an oligo synthesis cap, and is not credited.

**Disconfirmation attempt.** I checked the complete Python/Django surface, download scripts, tests, and embedded docs for length caps tied to synthesis, repeats/homopolymers, secondary structure, melting temperature, cross-hybridisation, or background-reference screening. None adds a qualifying class. I also tested whether restriction strings were merely appended; the post-assembly `findMatch` calls and documented warning header disconfirm that. What would change the score: a third distinct physical check, or a documented general QC operation, would support `yes`; removal of either implemented check would leave only narrow `partial` rather than `no`.

### 4. MPRA Design Tools — `partial` (high confidence)

**Primary evidence.** Audited `andrewGhazi/mpradesigntools` at commit [`afd386e`](https://github.com/andrewGhazi/mpradesigntools/tree/afd386ef12051bb0a37ad63a6f92acd555246584) and companion `designMPRA` at [`0cf56ee`](https://github.com/andrewGhazi/designMPRA/tree/0cf56ee602fc86dde705906d071a39cbdf6e99a8). The package manual documents three enzyme patterns, a `filterPatterns` barcode filter, a maximum construct size, and a two-table return in which `failed` records the SNP and reason ([processVCF.Rd lines 43–82](https://github.com/andrewGhazi/mpradesigntools/blob/afd386ef12051bb0a37ad63a6f92acd555246584/man/processVCF.Rd#L43-L82)). It specifically explains that `enzyme3` must be checked because an aberrant occurrence can make plasmid-library preparation fail ([lines 100–107](https://github.com/andrewGhazi/mpradesigntools/blob/afd386ef12051bb0a37ad63a6f92acd555246584/man/processVCF.Rd#L100-L107)).

Implementation matches the documentation: `max_construct_size` detects excess total length, trims context, and records a note ([processVCFfast.R lines 249–324](https://github.com/andrewGhazi/mpradesigntools/blob/afd386ef12051bb0a37ad63a6f92acd555246584/R/processVCFfast.R#L249-L324)); construct context with digestion sites returns an explicit failure reason when repair is disabled ([lines 344–372](https://github.com/andrewGhazi/mpradesigntools/blob/afd386ef12051bb0a37ad63a6f92acd555246584/R/processVCFfast.R#L344-L372)); and requested patterns plus all three enzyme sites are removed from the usable barcode pool ([lines 1184–1209](https://github.com/andrewGhazi/mpradesigntools/blob/afd386ef12051bb0a37ad63a6f92acd555246584/R/processVCFfast.R#L1184-L1209)). The companion app independently tells users that SNPs may fail because context contains or generates an undesired restriction site and renders a failed-SNP table ([ui.R lines 119–154](https://github.com/andrewGhazi/designMPRA/blob/0cf56ee602fc86dde705906d071a39cbdf6e99a8/ui.R#L119-L154)).

**Reasoning.** Restriction-site checking and maximum construct length are qualifying physical classes. The latter also repairs by trimming, but the note explicitly exposes the detected excess, satisfying the independent-exposure condition at the row-11 boundary. The arbitrary barcode pattern list is at most the row's generic predicate-like case; its default polyadenylation motif is biological content and does not add a physical class. With only a few physical classes, the consistent score is `partial`.

**Disconfirmation attempt.** I searched both repositories for GC-runtime checks, homopolymer/repeat checks, secondary structure, Tm, external background matching, and general post-design QC. The shipped barcode data are described as prefiltered, but importing a prebuilt set is not a checker supplied over arbitrary designs. What would change the score: independent named GC/repeat/structure checks over generated constructs would raise this to `yes`; if the documented digest failures were merely app prose, it would fall, but the package implementation confirms them.

### 5. Oligopool Calculator — `yes` (high confidence)

**Primary evidence.** Audited `ayaanhossain/oligopool` release [`v2026.02.22.1`](https://github.com/ayaanhossain/oligopool/tree/b88fa394ca67ed4c48ec41127b5707694ee7cf0a) / commit `b88fa394`. The package has a dedicated public `verify` operation—not a composition the caller must build—which says it checks an oligo pool for integrity, length, excluded-motif emergence, and background k-mer conflicts and returns per-row conflict flags/details ([verify.py lines 14–55](https://github.com/ayaanhossain/oligopool/blob/b88fa394ca67ed4c48ec41127b5707694ee7cf0a/oligopool/verify.py#L14-L55)). The external-reference behavior is explicit: a conflict is flagged when any oligo k-mer matches any supplied background database ([lines 49–63](https://github.com/ayaanhossain/oligopool/blob/b88fa394ca67ed4c48ec41127b5707694ee7cf0a/oligopool/verify.py#L49-L63)).

The implementation assembles `HasLengthConflict`, `HasExmotifConflict`, `HasBackgroundConflict`, `HasAnyConflicts`, and matching detail columns per oligo ([lines 190–220](https://github.com/ayaanhossain/oligopool/blob/b88fa394ca67ed4c48ec41127b5707694ee7cf0a/oligopool/verify.py#L190-L220)), then returns aggregate counts, the limit/motif/background parameters, per-column attribution, and a clean/conflict status ([lines 224–267](https://github.com/ayaanhossain/oligopool/blob/b88fa394ca67ed4c48ec41127b5707694ee7cf0a/oligopool/verify.py#L224-L267)). Its own command catalog labels this QC mode as detecting length, motif, and background conflicts ([descriptions.py lines 34–37 and 64–67](https://github.com/ayaanhossain/oligopool/blob/b88fa394ca67ed4c48ec41127b5707694ee7cf0a/oligopool/descriptions.py#L34-L67)).

**Reasoning.** Length cap, excluded physical/cloning motifs (including restriction sites when supplied), and external-background k-mer conflicts are three distinct named checks with per-oligo reporting. That meets `yes`. The score rests on `verify`, so it does not double-credit the package's design/repair operations.

**Disconfirmation attempt.** I checked whether the evidence came only from barcode/motif/primer design loops. It does not: `verify(input_data, ...)` accepts a completed oligo table and independently returns the violation matrix. I also checked whether background input merely seeded generation; `verify` opens the background databases and emits per-row flags/details. What would change the score: without the independent `verify` API, integrated avoidance during element design would require a row-11 boundary analysis and could score lower.

### 6. Mutation Maker — `no` (high confidence)

**Primary evidence.** Audited `ra100/Mutation_Maker` at commit [`396c1c0`](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/tree/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043). The active API's three design endpoints are explicitly `find_ssm_primers`, `find_qclm_primers`, and `find_pas_primers` ([server_fastapi.py lines 36–65](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/api/server_fastapi.py#L36-L65)); their tasks dispatch directly to the three primer-design solvers ([tasks.py lines 23–73](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/tasks.py#L23-L73)).

The strongest apparent counterevidence is primer-specific: the SSM solver calculates hairpin, homodimer, and heterodimer penalties for primer pairs ([ssm.py lines 82–146](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/ssm.py#L82-L146)), and primer outputs contain sequence, length, 3'-end temperature, GC content, and an in-range flag ([lines 742–803](https://github.com/ra100/Mutation_Maker/blob/396c1c0ede7529f3dedf65e821e8c1f20c9a7043/backend/mutation_maker/ssm.py#L742-L803)).

**Reasoning.** Those calculations select and describe the **primers the tool emits**. Crediting them here would double-credit the row-20 primer-design mechanism. No endpoint or documented function checks the designed variant-library sequences themselves for synthesis/cloning constraints. Therefore row 19 is `no`, while this finding makes no claim about row 20.

**Disconfirmation attempt.** I actively treated GC/Tm/in-range and dimer/hairpin code as possible positives, then traced every active API route and solver output. All such checks are nested in SSM/QCLM/PAS primer production; repository-wide searches found no separate construct/library QC operation, predicate API, restriction/repeat checker, or background-reference screen. What would change the score: a public operation accepting completed library sequences and returning/filtering physical violations independently of primer design.

### 7. DNA Chisel — `yes` (high confidence)

**Primary evidence.** Audited `Edinburgh-Genome-Foundry/DnaChisel` release [`v3.2.16`](https://github.com/Edinburgh-Genome-Foundry/DnaChisel/tree/68c09304341c3656f3dfe63eda37757d6a7b3917) / commit `68c0930`. The documented built-ins include `AvoidMatches`, `AvoidHairpins`, `AvoidHeterodimerization`, `AvoidPattern`, `EnforceGCContent`, `EnforceMeltingTemperature`, `SequenceLengthBounds`, and `UniquifyAllKmers` ([builtin_specifications.rst lines 9–32](https://github.com/Edinburgh-Genome-Foundry/DnaChisel/blob/68c09304341c3656f3dfe63eda37757d6a7b3917/docs/ref/builtin_specifications.rst#L9-L32)). The README connects these specifications to commercial-provider constraints, homology avoidance, GC, restriction-site avoidance, and independent pass summaries ([README.rst lines 20–31 and 47–76](https://github.com/Edinburgh-Genome-Foundry/DnaChisel/blob/68c09304341c3656f3dfe63eda37757d6a7b3917/README.rst#L20-L76)); it also documents Bowtie-backed `AvoidMatches` against existing genomes ([lines 177–188](https://github.com/Edinburgh-Genome-Foundry/DnaChisel/blob/68c09304341c3656f3dfe63eda37757d6a7b3917/README.rst#L177-L188)).

Most importantly for the row-11 boundary, `constraints_breaches_dataframe(constraints, sequences)` is a separate reporter over a sequence collection. Its documented example applies `AvoidPattern`, `EnforceGCContent`, and `UniquifyAllKmers` and writes a breach spreadsheet without resolving constraints ([constraints_breaches_dataframe.py lines 30–76](https://github.com/Edinburgh-Genome-Foundry/DnaChisel/blob/68c09304341c3656f3dfe63eda37757d6a7b3917/dnachisel/reports/constraints_reports/constraints_breaches_dataframe.py#L30-L76)); implementation evaluates every supplied constraint for every supplied sequence ([lines 79–101](https://github.com/Edinburgh-Genome-Foundry/DnaChisel/blob/68c09304341c3656f3dfe63eda37757d6a7b3917/dnachisel/reports/constraints_reports/constraints_breaches_dataframe.py#L79-L101)).

**Reasoning.** DNA Chisel has well over three named physical classes and an independently exposed checking/reporting interface, so it earns `yes` here even though the same specifications can also drive repair in row 11. Biological specifications such as translation/stop-codon checks were excluded from the count.

**Disconfirmation attempt.** I tried to show that all evaluations were inseparable from `resolve_constraints()`. The standalone breaches DataFrame API directly disproves that: it takes arbitrary sequences and reports locations without mutation. I also excluded generic custom `Specification` subclasses from the `yes`; the named built-ins alone suffice. What would change the score: removal of the independent evaluation/report APIs would force reconsideration under the row-11 no-double-credit boundary.

### 8. tangermeme — `no` (high confidence)

**Primary evidence.** Audited `jmschrei/tangermeme` at commit [`2006b31`](https://github.com/jmschrei/tangermeme/tree/2006b310cd72a28c56c3ea4ba67f738fff74bb89) (`v1.4.0-7-g2006b31`). The package describes itself as assumption-free sequence-model analysis/design with caller-supplied loss functions ([README lines 5–12](https://github.com/jmschrei/tangermeme/blob/2006b310cd72a28c56c3ea4ba67f738fff74bb89/README.md#L5-L12)). It does provide `gc_content`, but that function only returns a fraction per sequence; it has no permissible range, violation flag, rejection, or physical interpretation ([utils.py lines 896–935](https://github.com/jmschrei/tangermeme/blob/2006b310cd72a28c56c3ea4ba67f738fff74bb89/tangermeme/utils.py#L896-L935)).

The design API likewise uses an arbitrary model loss to choose substitutions ([greedy_substitution.py lines 24–53 and 84–109](https://github.com/jmschrei/tangermeme/blob/2006b310cd72a28c56c3ea4ba67f738fff74bb89/tangermeme/design/greedy_substitution.py#L24-L109)); `screen` ranks random sequences by predictive-model activity/loss, not physical realizability ([screen.py lines 19–44 and 63–83](https://github.com/jmschrei/tangermeme/blob/2006b310cd72a28c56c3ea4ba67f738fff74bb89/tangermeme/design/screen.py#L19-L83)).

**Reasoning.** A metric plus a generic model-loss optimizer is not a physical check. To obtain row-19 behavior, the caller would have to author a physical scoring/checking model and reconcile its outputs—user reconstruction under the global rule. `no` is therefore determinate.

**Disconfirmation attempt.** I searched every top-level module plus the full docs/tests for restriction sites, homopolymers/repeats, synthesis length, structure/hairpins/dimers, Tm, cloning, and external off-target checking. GC-matched genomic-background sampling in `match.py` is a biological-control selection method, not checking designed sequences for synthesis. What would change the score: a documented predicate/filter over input sequences, or named physical thresholds producing violation/rejection results.

## Final consistency and boundary check

- **Same threshold:** `yes` is used only for three or more named physical classes (PoolParty, Oligopool Calculator, DNA Chisel); one/two classes or a narrow/generic mechanism is `partial` (VaLiAnT, MPRAnator, MPRA Design Tools).
- **Detection vs repair (row 11):** PoolParty and VaLiAnT reject/filter; Oligopool's score rests on standalone `verify`; DNA Chisel's rests on standalone breaches reporting. MPRA Design Tools receives only `partial` because its length repair exposes the detection through a note and its restriction checks independently report/reject failures.
- **Detection vs primers (row 20):** Mutation Maker's primer thermodynamics are not credited here. No tool is credited in row 19 merely for emitting primer sequences or selecting primers.
- **External reference:** Oligopool's background k-mer databases and DNA Chisel's `AvoidMatches` qualify. VaLiAnT background VCFs do not: they alter biological reference context rather than screen oligos for physical off-target matching.
- **Biological-content exclusion:** stop-codon/frame/translation checks, VCF biological filters, MPRA polyadenylation-motif semantics, and tangermeme predictive-model activity were not counted.
- All eight values use the allowed vocabulary; no `unknown` was needed because each relevant public surface was determinate.
